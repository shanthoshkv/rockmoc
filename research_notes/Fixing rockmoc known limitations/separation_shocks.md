## Q1. Free-shock separation (FSS) criteria: exact formulas, which agrees best; FSS vs RSS, cap shock, side loads

### Takeaway
All one-line criteria (p_i/p_a as function of M_i or p0/p_a) carry large scatter (NASA advised a 20 % margin); Schmucker (1.88M−1)^−0.64 is the European industry default but DLR's own data show it under-predicts separation location (separation occurs further downstream / at lower p_sep/p_a); Stark's 2005 fit p_sep/p_a = π/(3·M_sep) is the simplest data-based alternative. Physically better is the two-step split p_i/p_a = (p_i/p_p)(p_p/p_a), with p_i/p_p from a shock/free-interaction correlation and p_p/p_a ≈ 0.9 (FSS, contoured nozzle) — but no general model for p_p/p_a exists for contoured nozzles. RSS only occurs in nozzles with an internal shock (TOP/TOC/CTIC), triggered by the cap-shock pattern.

### Cited Findings
Notation: subscript i = incipient separation (wall-pressure minimum, start of interaction) ≈ "sep"; p = plateau; a = ambient; M_i = inviscid wall Mach number at separation; p0 = pc = chamber pressure.

**p_i/p_a criteria (lump shock + recirculation recovery):**
- Summerfield (1954): p_i/p_a ≈ 0.4 (derived from conical nozzles, late 1940s) — [Östlund 2004 Eq.19](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf); Stark lists "Summerfield: p_sep/p_a = 0.35…0.4" — [Stark 2005 appendix](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf). TP-1207 notes 0.4 "is still quoted today… although more recent studies have shown it to be inadequate" — [NASA TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf).
- Schilling (1962): p_i/p_a = k1·(p0/p_a)^k2, k1 = 0.582, k2 = −0.195 (contoured); k1 = 0.541, k2 = −0.136 (conical) — [Östlund Eq.20](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Stark gives 0.583·(pc/pa)^−0.195 "suggested for short bell nozzles" (0.582 vs 0.583 discrepancy between sources) — [Stark 2005](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf).
- Kalt & Badal (1965): same form with k1 = 2/3, k2 = −0.2, i.e. p_i/p_a = (2/3)(p0/p_a)^−0.2 — [Östlund](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf); Stark appendix writes it as p_sep/p_a = (2/3)(pc/pa)^(−1/5) — [Stark 2005](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf).
- Kalt & Badal is what RocketCEA's `separated_Cf.ambientCf` uses (verified numerically by me: LOX/LH2, Pc=500 psia, Pa=14.7 → Psep=4.84 psia = (2/3)(34.0)^−0.2·14.7; Pc=1000 → Psep=4.214 psia). Their docs show eps=40, Pc=1000 psia: CFcea(vac-optimum ideal, pa-corrected) 1.78854 → CFamb separated 1.45502, epsSep=27.32 — [RocketCEA docs](https://rocketcea.readthedocs.io/en/stable/ambient_isp.html).
- Schmucker (1973, TU München report TB-7/-10/-14): p_i/p_a = (1.88·M_i − 1)^−0.64; "purely empirical", similar behaviour to Crocco–Probstein, "still widely used" — [Östlund Eq.21](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Called "the most widely used separation criterion today in the European space industry" — [Östlund appended GSTP paper, same PDF](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). (Note: one patent attributes this same formula to Summerfield — that is a misattribution per Östlund/Stark.)
- NASA 1976 (SP-8120 era) recommendation: Crocco–Probstein integral boundary-layer criterion with an additional 20 % margin on predicted separation — [Östlund](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- Stark (DLR, 2005), fit to an enlarged literature database plus DLR cold/hot tests: **p_sep/p_a = π / (3·M_sep)**, M_sep = wall Mach number at incipient separation (I verified the glyphs in the PDF: U+03C0 π and U+22C5 dot). The "p_sep/p_a = 1/M" in the prompt is this criterion (π/3 ≈ 1.047). Stark: cold-flow data "clearly fall below the expected trend… common separation criteria like Schmucker's under predict the separation location"; "An influence of the nozzle design [bell/conical/planar] on separation is not given"; wall Mach number captures the γ (propellant) effect; attached tubes delay separation, film cooling causes premature separation — [Stark 2005](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf).
- Morrisette & Goldberg (NASA TP-1207, 1978), axisymmetric contoured nozzles, 2nd-order fit valid 2.4 ≤ M_s ≤ 4.5: **p_s/p_a = 1.082 − 0.363·M_s + 0.0386·M_s²**. Conical-nozzle separation correlation independent of half-angle above ~9°; contoured data fall below Reshotko–Tucker theory for M_s > ~3 — [TP-1207 Eq.10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf).
- TP-1207 "exit deviation" (end effect) bounds — the correlation is invalid once the separation point is near the exit: A_s/A* ≈ 0.8·A_e/A* (Eq.7, rough guide above M=2) or better A_s/A* = 0.8(A_e/A* − 1) + 1 (Eq.8). Full-flowing (no separation, oblique shock at lip) when **p_t/p_a ≥ 1 + 0.39·(p_t/p_e)** (Eq.9) — [TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf). Östlund likewise: reversal of trend once local area ratio reaches ~80 % of exit value (Sunley & Ferriman "end effect") — [Östlund Sec.4.1.1](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- Arens & Spiegler (1963): first Mach-dependent criterion, based on Gadd's assumption that the separation pressure rise must stagnate a characteristic boundary-layer velocity (u/u_s = 0.6 suggested); "too complex for engineering application" — [Östlund](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf), [TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf). Reshotko & Tucker: Mach-number ratio across separation 0.762 (zero pressure gradient) — [TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf).

**p_i/p_p criteria (shock/boundary-layer interaction only):**
- Zukoski: p_i/p_p = (1 + 0.5·M_i)^−1, M_i = 1.4–6.0, Re > 1e5; agrees with plateau data in overexpanded conical nozzles M_i = 2.0–5.5; valid only for γ = 1.4 (air) — [Östlund Eq.22](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Stark lists the equivalent forward-facing-step form p_sep/p = 2/(M_sep + 2) — [Stark 2005](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf).
- Summerfield oblique-shock variant: separated-flow deflection angle ≈ 15° constant; compute p_p/p_i from oblique-shock theory with local γ — [Östlund](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- Östlund (VOLVO data), 2.5 ≤ M_i ≤ 4.5: p_i/p_p = [1 + γ·M_i²·sin²β·(1 − tan(β−θ)/tan β)]^−1 with θ = −3.764·M_i + 42.878 [deg], β = 1.678·M_i + 9.347 [deg] — [Östlund Eq.23 / Stark appendix](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). (Equation text was garbled in extraction; I reconstructed it as the exact oblique-shock pressure-jump identity, which matches both garbled copies — verify against the PDF figure before coding. Note β and θ labels: Stark lists "−3,764 Masep + 42,878" and "1,678 Masep + 9,347" without readable symbols; check which is β vs θ. A shock angle of ~32° at M=3 vs deflection ~14.4° — physically β = −3.764M + 42.878 (≈31.6° at M=3) and θ = 1.678M + 9.347 (≈14.4°) is the self-consistent assignment, i.e. possibly the reverse of what my reading of Östlund's text gives. FLAG.)
- Frey (2001 thesis): p_i/p_p = [1 + (2γ/(γ+1))·(M_i²·sin²β − 1)]^−1 with β = 44.5 − 4.7·M_i [deg], 2.5 ≤ M_i ≤ 4.5; gives wrong trend above M ≈ 4.8 (minimum then rise) — [Östlund Eq.24](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf), confirmed in [Stark appendix](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf). (β = 44.5 − 4.7·3 = 30.4° at M=3 — consistent with the β assignment above.)
- Carrière generalized free-interaction theory (non-uniform flow, wall curvature): F(s, p') = (p(x)−p_i)/(q_i·√(C_fi)) ·… with F_s = 4.22 at separation, F_p = 6.00 at plateau; Östlund reformulated it into an implicit separation criterion and fitted interaction length l_p/δ*_i to VOLVO S6, validated on VOLVO S7 — [Östlund Sec.5.1.3](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf) (needs boundary-layer δ*, C_f; beyond an inviscid MOC library).

**p_p/p_a (recirculation recovery):**
- Kudryavtsev (conical nozzles, air, half-angle α < 15°): p_a/p_p = 1 + (0.192/sin α − 0.7)·(1 − M_sep/M_des); for α > 15°, p_p/p_a ≈ 1 — [Stark appendix](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf), [Östlund Eq.28](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf) (garbled; reconstructed from both copies — FLAG).
- "Such a model is currently not available for contoured nozzles"; p_p/p_a depends on separated-region length, downstream wall curvature, radial gap between jet and wall — [Östlund Sec.5.1.4](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- GSTP/VOLVO parabolic subscale: p_p/p_a ≈ 0.9 for FSS and 0.7–0.85 for RSS depending on separation position; hence Schmucker "far too simple" — [Östlund appended GSTP paper](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Guman suggested p_p ≈ 0.65·p_a as correlating parameter for contoured nozzles — [TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf).
- Stark counter-view: dichotomy not needed for design; one criterion giving the lowest achievable wall pressure suffices — [Stark 2005](https://elib.dlr.de/49253/1/AIAA2005-3940.pdf).

**FSS vs RSS, cap shock, side loads:**
- FSS: jet separates fully; wall pressure: attached → steep rise (i→p) → slow rise to slightly below p_a at exit due to ambient inflow — [Östlund Sec.4.1](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- RSS discovered in J-2S subscale cold-flow tests (Nave & Coffey 1973); separated flow reattaches → closed recirculation bubble, wall pressures can exceed p_a downstream, alternating shocks/expansions — [Östlund Sec.4.2](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- Frey & Hagemann: FSS→RSS transition driven by the cap-shock pattern (interference of separation shock with inverse Mach reflection of the internal shock at the centreline, trapped vortex behind it); "a transition from FSS to RSS can only occur in nozzles featuring an internal shock" — [Östlund Sec.4.2.4](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Nasuti & Onofri alternative: centreline vortex pushes jet toward wall — same source. Key refs: Frey & Hagemann, "Restricted Shock Separation in Rocket Nozzles", JPP 16(3):478–484, 2000, doi:10.2514/2.5593; Frey & Hagemann 1999 abstract: major side load in TOP nozzles due to FSS↔RSS transition — [DLR elib 1416/1417](https://elib.dlr.de/1416/) (via search snippet, not read in full).
- TIC and conical nozzles show only FSS; TOP shows both — [Martelli et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08500); [Frey et al. EUCASS 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-359.pdf).
- TICTOP (ideal contour then parabola): fully shock-free TICTOP-B1/B2 never showed RSS; TICTOP-C2 with "moderate compression" showed RSS in 2 of 16 shutdowns; DLR-TOP showed RSS in half the shutdowns under deviated (condensation) conditions; RSS side loads ~3× FSS; "a TICTOP nozzle must be completely shock-free to suppress RSS" — [Frey et al. EUCASS 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-359.pdf).
- Side-load model: Schmucker tilted-separation-line model (also P&W, Rocketdyne, Aerojet variants); Östlund concluded "the Schmucker model is too simple" — [Östlund Sec.5.2.1 / list p.~10](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). FSS side loads largest at n = 0.05–0.25 in VOLVO S6 — same source.

### Inferences
- For rockmoc (inviscid, has wall M(x), p_w(x) from MOC): implement a pluggable `p_sep/p_a = f(M_w)` with options {Summerfield 0.4, Schmucker, Stark π/(3M), Morrisette–Goldberg quadratic (contoured), Kalt–Badal/Schilling (p0/pa forms)}; separation x is the first wall station where p_w(x) ≤ f(M_w(x))·p_a. Default to Schmucker (industry standard) and report Stark as a bracket; show both since they differ (Stark predicts lower p_sep/p_a → separation further downstream). Numerically at M=3: Summerfield 0.40, Schmucker (4.64)^−0.64 = 0.375, Stark 0.349, M&G 0.341; at M=4: Schmucker 0.293, Stark 0.262, M&G 0.244 (my arithmetic).
- Apply the TP-1207 Eq.9 full-flow check (p0/p_a ≥ 1 + 0.39·p0/p_e ⇒ no separation) and the 0.8·A_e "exit deviation" zone as a warning band.
- Flag RSS risk (not predictable from criteria) whenever the MOC detects an internal shock (Q3) and the predicted separation point lies downstream of where the internal shock meets/approaches the axis-wall region; TIC/ideal-truncated contours → FSS only.

## Q2. Thrust with separation: realistic sea-level Cf of overexpanded nozzles

### Takeaway
Standard engineering model: integrate wall pressure of the attached (inviscid MOC) solution only up to x_sep, and take p_w ≈ p_a (or ≈ p_p ≈ 0.9 p_a rising to ~p_a at exit) downstream, so the downstream skirt contributes ~zero net axial force. Equivalently, Cf ≈ Cf of the nozzle truncated at ε_sep, evaluated at ambient p_a (this is what RocketCEA does with Kalt–Badal). Separation always improves Cf relative to the attached-flow prediction for the same overexpanded nozzle.

### Cited Findings
- Wall pressure downstream of separation: weak rise from p_p to "a wall pressure slightly below the ambient pressure" at the exit, caused by ambient-gas inflow into the recirculation zone — [Östlund Sec.4.1](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). Measured p_p/p_a ≈ 0.9 (FSS), 0.7–0.85 (RSS) in GSTP parabolic nozzle — [Östlund GSTP paper](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- In RSS, the closed recirculation zone has pressures "significantly below the ambient pressure level" and after reattachment wall pressures can exceed p_a; separation line jumps downstream at FSS→RSS — [Östlund Sec.4.2.1](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- RocketCEA separated-Cf example (Kalt–Badal): LOX/LH2 Pc=1000 psia, Pa=14.7 psia, ε=40: Cf 1.78854 (ideal) → 1.45502 separated, ε_sep = 27.32; Pc=500 psia: ε=20 and ε=50 both separate at ε_sep=14.2, IspAmb 316.16 s and 309.31 s respectively — [RocketCEA docs](https://rocketcea.readthedocs.io/en/stable/ambient_isp.html). (Note: the 1.789 here appears to be the CEA ambient-referenced value; the drop shown may combine separation with the pressure term — not fully documented.)
- MIT 16.50 (after Sutton & Biblarz): separated nozzle treated as a nozzle with effective exit pressure p_e' = 0.4·p_a — [MIT 16.50 lec7](https://mitocw.ups.edu.ec/courses/aeronautics-and-astronautics/16-50-introduction-to-propulsion-systems-spring-2012/lecture-notes/MIT16_50S12_lec7.pdf) (snippet).
- NASA 2D C-D nozzle tests: "In all cases, separation provided a significant increase in static thrust efficiency compared to the ideal prediction" (ideal = attached overexpanded) — [NASA NTRS 20040090513 (Hunter)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040090513/downloads/20040090513.pdf) (snippet).

### Inferences
- Algorithm for rockmoc (attached-flow wall solution known):
  1. x_sep = first wall station with p_w(x)/p_a ≤ f_crit(M_w(x)) (Q1). If none → attached, use existing integration.
  2. F = ṁ·u-momentum-flux + (p − p_a)·A at a cross-section is the existing approach; with separation, simplest consistent form: F_sep = F_vac,truncated(x_sep) − p_a·A_sep + ∫_{x_sep}^{x_e} (p_w − p_a) dA_wall, with p_w downstream modelled as (a) p_a (Summerfield-style, ∫ = 0), or (b) linear ramp from p_p = p_sep/f_ip(M) [Zukoski: p_p = p_sep·(1+0.5M)] capped at k·p_a (k≈0.9) up to ~p_a at exit. Option (a) gives Cf = Cf of truncated nozzle at p_a; (b) gives a small additional negative contribution (p_w < p_a on the skirt).
  3. Report a band using Schmucker and Stark separation points.
- For TOP nozzles flagged with internal shock, attach a "possible RSS: thrust/side-load not predicted" warning rather than modelling RSS.

- No published, tabulated measured sea-level Cf vs NPR data for a bell nozzle with separation found within budget (NASA TN D-467 Campbell & Farley conical nozzle performance data exist per Östlund/Stark references but not retrieved).

## Q3. Internal-shock prediction in MOC (TOP/parabolic, conical-to-bell)

### Takeaway
In TOP/Rao-parabola contours, the wall turns back (compression) after the initial expansion arc; the reflected/emitted waves of the same family (right-running, C−, heading toward the axis from the upper wall) converge and coalesce into a weak internal shock. The practical detection used by Frey et al. is visual/geometric convergence of right-running characteristics; in a MOC code this becomes a "characteristic crossing" test (same-family lines overtaking each other / grid Jacobian sign change).

### Cited Findings
- EUCASS 2017: "Converging characteristics indicate an internal shock in the DLR-TOP… and moderate compression in TICTOP-C2, whereas the right-running characteristics diverge or are parallel for TICTOP-B1, indeed confirming shock-free flow" — [Frey et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-359.pdf). Location of a wall-pressure increase can be found by following the left-running characteristic through the first occurrence of condensation — same source.
- TIC produces shock-free flow and only FSS; "there is always an internal shock in the TOP"; TIC vs TOP of same length/area ratio give same Isp but TOP has higher exit wall pressure (better separation margin) — [Frey et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-359.pdf).
- Internal shock "emanating from the inflection point of the contour" in TOP nozzles (Vulcain, SSME, J-2S, GSTP are "parabolic nozzles of Rao type with an internal shock induced in the throat region"); its shape and strength govern FSS→RSS transition — [Östlund thesis Sec.7.1.1 and GSTP paper](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).

### Inferences
- Simple detection algorithm for rockmoc (my design, not from a source):
  - Along each left-running C+ line k (or each marching front), the intersections with successive right-running C− lines j, j+1 must stay ordered (x_{j+1} > x_j, or monotonic arclength). Shock onset = first (j,k) where the cell area (Jacobian of x,r w.r.t. j,k indices) changes sign or goes below ε, i.e. adjacent C− lines cross.
  - Equivalent cheap pre-check at the wall: compute θ_w(x) of the given contour; if dθ_w/dx < 0 while the wall-Mach number along the wall decreases (dp_w/dx > 0) the wall is emitting compression waves → shock can form. Estimate coalescence distance: the intersection of neighbouring C− lines from the wall at slopes tan(θ − μ).
  - On detection: record shock origin (x,r), stop marching the crossed cells or insert a discontinuity (shock-fitting: apply oblique-shock jump with β from local M and flow-turning) — full shock-fitting not covered by sources found.
  - Outputs: boolean `has_internal_shock`, origin point, path, and "RSS-prone" flag (Q1).
- Conical-to-bell (CTIC) nozzles also have internal shocks per Östlund (CTIC listed with TOP as RSS-prone) — [Östlund Sec.4.2.3](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).

### Gaps
- No open source found giving a published coalescence algorithm or shock-fitting MOC for TOP nozzles (Hagemann/Frey use full Euler/RANS). Zucrow & Hoffman textbook MOC with shock fitting is standard but not retrieved here.
- Frey & Hagemann 2000 JPP full text not retrieved (paywalled).

## Q4. Validation data (wall pressure / separation location)

### Takeaway
Best openly accessible datasets are in Östlund's thesis (VOLVO/GSTP S1 parabolic subscale, S6 TIC, S7 short nozzle — plotted, not tabulated) and the DLR P6.2 TOP/TIC/TICTOP campaigns; usable numeric anchors are geometry and transition NPRs, not tabulated pressure tables.

### Cited Findings
- GSTP flow-separation-control reference nozzle (VOLVO, air, cold flow, "designed to resemble the separation and structure response of the Vulcain nozzle"): area ratio ε = 20, length L = 350 mm, throat radius r_t = 33.54 mm, exit radius r_e = 150 mm, design feed pressure 5.0 MPa, T0 = 450 K, gas: air. Steady wall pressure profiles for p0/p_a ≈ 10–50 (11.5, 14.2, 15.1, 16.2, 19.5, 22.0, 25.0, 26.5, 28.4, 40.5, 50.5) with Euler calc — [Östlund appended paper](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). (It is my inference that this GSTP reference nozzle is "VOLVO S1": the paper's figures refer to the S1 nozzle; verify.)
- VOLVO S1 (parabolic, internal shock): FSS for n < 0.14, RSS for n > 0.14 at start-up; RSS→FSS at n = 0.11 on shutdown (hysteresis); "end effect" (RSS re-transition, pulsating) at n ≈ 0.25; side-load peaks at n = 0.14/0.11 and 0.25 — [Östlund Sec.4.2](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). (n = "degree of overexpansion", likely p_e/p_a — definition not confirmed in extracted text; FLAG.)
- VOLVO S6 (truncated ideal, FSS only): side loads largest n = 0.05–0.25; used to fit Carrière interaction length, 2.82 ≤ M_i ≤ 3.25, −0.9 ≤ p'·10³ ≤ −0.5, n = 0.04–0.24. VOLVO S7 short nozzle: validation of Östlund criterion; statistical wall-pressure data at wall M = 3.8 — [Östlund Sec.4–5](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf).
- J-2S (Rocketdyne): full-scale 265 klbf, area ratio 40:1; Nave & Coffey 1973 "Sea level side loads in high-area-ratio rocket engines" developed empirical separation and side-load model from full-scale and model tests; RSS first observed in its subscale cold-flow tests — [NTRS 19740030190](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740030190); [Östlund](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf). J-2S side loads failed gimbal-block bolts in tension — [Shi, Boeing-Rocketdyne, NTRS 20050204019](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050204019/downloads/20050204019.pdf).
- DLR-TOP, TICTOP-B1/B2/C2 tested at DLR P6.2 (cold flow); TOP shows cap shock in Schlieren, TICTOP "Mach lens" — [Frey et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-359.pdf). Frey, Stark et al. 2000 "Subscale nozzle testing at the P6.2 test stand", AIAA 2000-3777 (36th JPC) — cited in [Östlund ref 43](https://www.mech.kth.se/thesis/2004/phd/phd_2004_jan_ostlund.pdf) (not retrieved).

- Morrisette & Goldberg compiled a multi-source table (Table I) of nozzle separation datasets with γ, geometry, Re and plotted p_s/p_a vs M_s for conical, 2D wedge, contoured nozzles (Figs 8–11) — [TP-1207](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780022611/downloads/19780022611.pdf). Schmucker TM X-64890 also tabulates hot-firing nozzles (P&W, NASA-MSFC 4k engine, etc.) with Pc, ε, wall type — [NASA TM X-64890](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19750003989/downloads/19750003989.pdf).
